## Title
Constitutional Membrane Networking: Proof-Carrying Agency at Scale

## Authors
Mohamed Beshr

## Abstract
We present Constitutional Membrane Networking (CMN), a network topology for distributed intelligence in which sovereign local agents interact with a single shared Universal Resource Pool (URP) through a fail-closed constitutional membrane. CMN addresses the Agentic Governance Trilemma: the impossibility of simultaneously achieving distribution, privacy, and behavioral guarantees in current architectures. Each human operator runs a Personal Agentic Team (PAT-7) locally; the shared URP accumulates System Agentic Teams (SAT-5) from every node. The membrane admits three outcomes — stay local, cross as a receipt-carrying event, or reject — making identity exposure topologically impossible. We formalize the membrane as a deterministic finite automaton, introduce Isnad Risk Propagation (IRP) from classical hadith science, present the Seed Chain as an autopoietic prompt architecture with monotone confidence propagation, and demonstrate Sippar exact arithmetic as one component of a compound non-extraction guarantee. Empirical evidence shows governance overhead under 5 microseconds (O(1)) and 126.7x latency reduction through governed self-improvement. The flywheel is topology-complete at N=1. We classify all claims by evidence level and explicitly state five non-theorems. Governance must reside above cognition, not beside it.
